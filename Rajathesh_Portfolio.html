Football Event Knowledge

Event Taxonomy

A working knowledge of football event definitions is the foundation of accurate data collection. These are the core events I identify, classify, and tag across matches.

Note: The event definitions shown below are simplified explanations based on my understanding of football data collection concepts and are intended for portfolio purposes.

Attacking
Pass
An intentional play by a player to direct the ball to a teammate. Classified by direction, length, body part, and outcome — completed or incomplete.
Attacking
Cross
A pass delivered from a wide area into the penalty box, typically targeting attacking players. Tagged by delivery type — open play, set piece, or cutback.
Attacking
Through Ball
A pass played into space behind the defensive line to release a teammate into a goalscoring position. Requires precise timing and spatial awareness to identify correctly.
Attacking
Assist
The final action — pass, cross, or touch — that directly leads to a goal being scored. Distinguished from secondary assists and chance-creation actions.
Shooting
Shot
Any deliberate attempt by a player to score a goal. Categorised by body part used, shot type (e.g. header, volley), and the resulting outcome — goal, saved, blocked, or off target.
Shooting
Shot on Target
A shot that is on course to enter the goal but is saved by the goalkeeper or stopped on the line. A key metric for measuring attacking threat and goalkeeping performance.
Defensive
Tackle
A physical challenge in which a defending player attempts to win the ball from an opponent. Tagged as successful (ball won) or unsuccessful, with location recorded.
Defensive
Interception
A defensive action where a player reads and cuts out a pass intended for an opponent, preventing the ball from reaching its target. Reflects positioning and anticipation.
Defensive
Clearance
A defensive action where a player removes the ball from a dangerous area near their own goal. Tagged by method — headed, with foot — and whether possession was retained.
Contested
Duel
A contested physical situation between two players — aerial or ground. Aerial duels involve heading contests; ground duels cover shoulder-to-shoulder challenges and tackles. Outcome recorded as won, lost, or draw.
Defensive
Recovery
A situation where a player regains possession of a loose ball with no direct opponent challenge. Indicates pressing effectiveness and defensive organisation when tracked at a team level.
Sports Data Operations

The Workflow

Every data point starts with a structured process. Here is how a sports data analyst moves from match assignment to final submission while maintaining the quality standards that downstream analytics depend on.

01
Match Assignment
Receive match file, competition details, and event specification guidelines for the fixture.
→
02
Video Review
Watch footage to understand the flow of the match, identify key phases, and prepare for tagging.
→
03
Event Tagging
Tag all match events — passes, shots, duels, and more — against standardised definitions with precise timestamps.
→
04
Quality Check
Review tagged output against the footage for accuracy, completeness, and consistency with event specifications.
→
✓
Final Submission
Submit verified, clean data ready for analytics platforms, broadcast systems, or club pipelines.
Why the process matters

Sports data is only as valuable as it is reliable. Throughout every stage of this workflow, consistency and accuracy are non-negotiable — a misclassified event, a missed timestamp, or an inconsistent definition can skew the metrics that coaches, analysts, and clubs use to make decisions. Through my experience at Hudl, I've developed the discipline to follow this process precisely, cross-reference my output against event specifications, and treat every match with the same standard of care regardless of competition level or sport.

Professional Development

What I've Learned in Sports Data Operations

Working in a professional sports data environment has taught me skills that go well beyond tagging. These are the principles that shape how I approach every piece of work.

🎯
Importance of Accuracy

In sports data, a single incorrect event can distort a player's statistics or a team's tactical profile. I've learned to work methodically — pausing, re-watching, and verifying before tagging — because accuracy is the baseline expectation, not an extra effort.

📐
Maintaining Consistency

When many analysts work across the same dataset, consistency is what makes the data trustworthy. I've developed the habit of applying the same definitions and decision-making framework regardless of the match, league, or operator I'm working alongside.

📖
Understanding Sport-Specific Rules

Each sport has its own set of rules, rhythms, and event types. Tagging football is fundamentally different from tagging basketball or volleyball. Building genuine sport-specific knowledge has made me a more confident and precise data collector across all three.

🔄
Working Across Multiple Sports

Switching contexts between football, basketball, and volleyball has built my adaptability. I've learned how to shift my mental model quickly, apply sport-specific frameworks, and maintain quality output across very different event structures and competition formats.

🌐
Remote Collaboration

Sports data operations at scale are distributed by nature. I've learned how to work independently while staying aligned with team standards, communicate clearly about edge cases, and contribute reliably to shared pipelines without direct supervision.

📈
Continuous Improvement

The event specifications and quality benchmarks in sports data evolve as the industry does. I've learned to treat feedback as a tool for growth, to revisit my own work critically, and to keep learning — whether that's a new event type, a new sport, or a new analytics concept.
